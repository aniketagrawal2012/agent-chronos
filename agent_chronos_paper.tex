\begin{abstract}
As enterprise deployments of autonomous agent swarms transition from single-tenant sandbox prototypes to heterogeneous multi-cloud topologies spanning Google Cloud Vertex AI, SAP S/4HANA, Salesforce Agentforce, and AWS Bedrock over Agent2Agent (A2A) and the Model Context Protocol (MCP), distributed consistency across external tool invocations degrades severely. Because Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning durations vary stochastically ($200\text{ ms}$ to $5{,}000\text{ ms}$) and wide-area network (WAN) transit jitter ($\pm 10\text{ ms}$ to $60\text{ ms}$) reorders packets in flight, multi-agent systems exhibit high rates of causal inversion ($19.50\%$) and phantom tool mutations ($14.80\%$) when upstream speculative reasoning branches abort after downstream mutating actions have already been dispatched. Traditional distributed database locks (Two-Phase Commit, XA) cannot be held across third-party REST and gRPC endpoints, while post-hoc compensating sagas fail when external side-effects are irreversible. We present \textbf{Agent-Chronos}, a hardware-anchored L7 transport fencing and speculative pacing protocol that guarantees strict client-side egress linearizability and end-to-end causal delivery across Chronos-enabled multi-agent gateways (and via a shared logical egress serialization gateway with sequential per-resource pipelining for unmodified third-party SaaS APIs) without distributed database locks. Agent-Chronos: (1) embeds physical TrueTime atomic/GPS clock uncertainty tuples $\langle t_{\text{earliest}}, t_{\text{latest}}, \epsilon_u \rangle$, swarm-wide causal vectors $\mathcal{V}$, and per-destination sequence coordinates $\text{Seq}_{\text{dest}}$ directly into HTTP/2 framing; (2) implements a two-phase user-space L7 egress pacer (prior to HPACK compression and TLS encryption) that stages speculative mutations during upstream verification ($\Delta_{\text{stage}}$) and enforces a post-settlement TrueTime commit-wait barrier ($\Delta_{\text{wait}} = \max(0, t_{F,\text{latest}} + \Delta_{\text{abort\_prop}} - t_{\text{now},\text{earliest}})$) before wire release; (3) enforces an ingress causal hold barrier at receiving sidecars to neutralize WAN transit jitter while permitting dependency-free read-only queries to bypass pacing with $0.00\text{ ms}$ delay; and (4) provides pre-wire speculative revocation that purges aborted frames in user-space gateway queues so zero mutating payload bytes reach downstream endpoints. Across $N = 1{,}000$ multi-agent transactions per seed ($5$ seeds, $5{,}000$ total transaction runs per baseline) in an emulated cross-cloud testbed, Agent-Chronos reduced phantom mutations and causal inversions to $0.00\%$, bounding post-settlement TrueTime commit-wait pacing overhead to a mean of $5.91 \pm 2.32\text{ ms}$ ($p_{50} = 5.90\text{ ms}$, $p_{95} = 9.80\text{ ms}$, $p_{99} = 10.84\text{ ms}$) while achieving a net end-to-end speculative speedup of $423.3\text{ ms}$ ($34.6\%$) per committed transaction ($388.5\text{ ms}$, or $31.8\%$, unconditionally across all transactions including aborted branch retries) over non-speculative sequential execution. All protocol transitions are verifiable via deterministic HTTP/2 headers and audit trailers, providing end-to-end cross-organization protocol observability.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Autonomous multi-agent systems have emerged as a primary architectural paradigm for enterprise generative AI orchestration. Modern enterprise workflows coordinate swarms of specialized agents executing complex multi-turn objectives---such as reconciling financial ledgers, assessing procurement risk, and updating Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) records across heterogeneous cloud boundaries~\cite{mcp2024spec, a2a2025spec, yao2024taubench}.

However, as agent swarms expand across organizational and cloud boundaries, a critical distributed systems failure mode arises: \textbf{Speculative Causal Inversion and Phantom Tool Mutations}. To hide multi-second LLM generation latencies, modern agentic serving runtimes speculatively synthesize and stage downstream tool calls in parallel with upstream policy verification and sibling reasoning branches~\cite{nightingale2005speculator, patil2024goex, chang2025sagallm, ye2025speculative, ro2025sherlock, mohammadi2026atomix}. Unlike deterministic microservices whose execution latency is tightly bounded, LLM reasoning and guardrail verification turns vary stochastically from $200\text{ ms}$ to over $5{,}000\text{ ms}$. Concurrently, regional WAN jitter across public interconnects ($\pm 10\text{ ms}$ to $60\text{ ms}$) reorders packets in flight. When a downstream agent dispatches an irreversible external mutation (e.g., executing an electronic funds transfer or releasing an ERP inventory hold) before an upstream speculative epoch settles---or when clock skew ($\pm \epsilon_u$) across distributed verification nodes causes an upstream abort signal and a downstream commit candidate to cross in flight---any subsequent upstream policy abort leaves a \emph{phantom mutation} on the destination system of record.

Existing distributed transaction and coordination primitives do not fully resolve this boundary gap:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Classical Distributed Locks, Agentic Sagas, and Logical Pre-Commit Staging:} Two-Phase Commit (2PC) and XA locks cannot be held across stateless third-party SaaS REST/gRPC APIs (e.g., Stripe, SAP NetWeaver, or Salesforce). Classical compensating sagas~\cite{garcia1987sagas}, multi-agent workflow validation coordinators such as SagaLLM~\cite{chang2025sagallm} and ALAS~\cite{geng2025alas}, and post-facto undo runtimes such as GoEX~\cite{patil2024goex} operate at the application orchestration layer without hardware-clock transport fencing. Similarly, recent speculative and transactional agent runtimes---including Speculative Actions~\cite{ye2025speculative}, Sherlock~\cite{ro2025sherlock}, and Atomix~\cite{mohammadi2026atomix}---overlap verification with logical pre-commit staging and read-only bypass inside a single centralized orchestrator. However, when such logical pre-commit staging designs are deployed across \emph{decentralized cross-node verification topologies} (modeled by our \emph{Chronos Vector-Only Ablation} in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}), logical staging alone---without a hardware-anchored $2\epsilon_{\text{eff}}$ commit-wait barrier---leaks phantom mutations ($2.90\%$ in Table~\ref{tab:benchmark_results}) when distributed cross-node verification aborts race within the physical clock-skew window and incurs destination causal inversions ($3.40\%$) across concurrent sibling branches ($\mathcal{V}_1 \parallel \mathcal{V}_2$).
    \item \textbf{OS Speculative Buffering and NIC Pacers:} Operating-system speculative execution frameworks such as Speculator~\cite{nightingale2005speculator} buffer uncommitted kernel outputs inside a single OS domain, while end-host traffic shapers such as SENIC~\cite{radhakrishnan2014senic} and Carousel~\cite{saeed2017carousel} pace NIC packet departure rates for bandwidth shaping. However, raw kernel TCP socket buffering (\texttt{sk\_buff}) cannot selectively excise individual aborted tool frames multiplexed over a shared HTTP/2-over-TLS (\texttt{h2}) connection without corrupting stateful HPACK header tables, TLS 1.3 AEAD record sequence numbers, and TCP byte-stream sequence offsets.
    \item \textbf{Logical and Hybrid Clocks Under Commodity NTP Skew:} Classical Lamport timestamps~\cite{lamport1978time}, Fidge/Mattern vector clocks~\cite{fidge1988timestamps, mattern1988virtual}, and $\mathcal{O}(1)$-space Hybrid Logical Clocks (HLCs)~\cite{kulkarni2014hlc} track message-passing causality, but under commodity NTP synchronization ($\epsilon_{\text{NTP}} \approx 25\text{--}100\text{ ms}$) they incur wide physical uncertainty windows when enforcing real-time commit-wait barriers across independent gateways, while scalar HLCs cannot distinguish concurrent sibling branch forks without explicit per-agent vector coordinates.
\end{enumerate}

To address these challenges, we introduce \textbf{Agent-Chronos}, an L7 transport fencing protocol that bridges hardware-anchored TrueTime uncertainty intervals~\cite{corbett2013spanner} with modern agentic protocols (MCP~\cite{mcp2024spec} via FastMCP gateways and A2A~\cite{a2a2025spec}). Rather than claiming database-internal strict serializability over unmodified third-party storage engines that lack two-phase locking or Paxos consensus, Agent-Chronos formalizes and enforces \textbf{strict client-side egress linearizability}~\cite{herlihy1990linearizability} (guaranteeing that no speculative mutation departs the egress gateway until its upstream epoch has deterministically settled and its physical TrueTime uncertainty and abort-propagation window has elapsed) together with \textbf{end-to-end causal delivery} across Chronos-enabled ingress sidecars (e.g., fronting enterprise SAP S/4HANA gateways). When invoking unmodified external third-party SaaS endpoints (such as Stripe) that cannot host an ingress sidecar, the sender-side gateway suppresses tombstone sequence ACKs, coordinates concurrent sibling writes ($\mathcal{V}_1 \parallel \mathcal{V}_2$) by routing all mutations for a given SaaS target through a shared logical egress serialization gateway shard, and falls back to sequential per-resource in-flight pipelining ($+42.0\text{ ms}$ mean per-transaction overhead) paired with compensating sagas for post-transmission HTTP 5xx faults.

\section{System Architecture and Formal Invariants}
\label{sec:architecture}
Agent-Chronos operates as a user-space L7 HTTP/2 frame-staging filter embedded at the egress and ingress boundaries of agent runtimes---specifically Google Agent Development Kit (ADK) workers, FastMCP gateways, and Envoy sidecar proxies---prior to stateful HTTP/2 HPACK compression, TLS 1.3 record encryption, and kernel TCP \texttt{sendmsg()} socket calls.

\subsection{TrueTime Uncertainty Representation and Multi-Cloud Clock Model}
Let $\mathbb{N}_0 = \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}$ denote the non-negative integers and $\mathbb{N}^+ = \{1, 2, \dots\}$ denote the strictly positive integers. Let $t_{\text{real}} \in \mathbb{N}^+$ denote absolute physical coordinate time expressed in discrete integer nanoseconds since the Unix epoch. On any node $u$, a hardware-anchored TrueTime provider~\cite{corbett2013spanner} backed by GPS receivers and rubidium atomic clocks exposes a closed physical time uncertainty interval around node $u$'s local reading $t_{\text{now},u} = t_{\text{real}} + \delta_u$ (where $\delta_u \in [-\epsilon_u, +\epsilon_u]$ is node $u$'s instantaneous signed physical clock offset):
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
T_{\text{now},u} &= [t_{\text{now},u,\text{earliest}},\, t_{\text{now},u,\text{latest}}], \\
t_{\text{now},u,\text{latest}} - t_{\text{now},u,\text{earliest}} &= 2\epsilon_u
\end{split}
\label{eq:truetime_interval}
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon_u \in \mathbb{N}^+$ represents node $u$'s instantaneous local clock drift bound ($\epsilon_u \in [1, 7]\text{ ms}$ in production Google Cloud data centers). For wire serialization of a single node's raw clock sample $T_{\text{now},u}$, Agent-Chronos encodes the 3-tuple $\mathcal{W}(T_{\text{now},u}) = \langle t_{\text{now},u,\text{earliest}}, t_{\text{now},u,\text{latest}}, \epsilon_u \rangle$, where receivers verify the exact single-node framing identity $t_{\text{now},u,\text{latest}} - t_{\text{now},u,\text{earliest}} = 2\epsilon_u$. In particular, when an upstream verification node settles epoch $E$, its intra-cluster settlement token transmits $\mathcal{W}(T_E) = \langle t_{E,\text{earliest}}, t_{E,\text{latest}}, \epsilon_{\text{verifier}} \rangle$ to the egress gateway, which uses $\epsilon_{\text{verifier}}$ and its local $\epsilon_{\text{gateway}}$ to compose the heterogeneous cross-node uncertainty bound $\epsilon_{\text{eff}} = \lceil (\epsilon_{\text{verifier}} + \epsilon_{\text{gateway}})/2 \rceil \in \mathbb{N}^+$ (ceiling-rounded to preserve conservative integer nanosecond header encoding when $\epsilon_{\text{verifier}}$ and $\epsilon_{\text{gateway}}$ have opposite parity, while the Phase~2 barrier in Eq.~\eqref{eq:pacing_wait} evaluates the exact integer sum $2\epsilon_{\text{eff}}^{\text{exact}} = \epsilon_{\text{verifier}} + \epsilon_{\text{gateway}} \in \mathbb{N}^+$; for the composite outbound settlement header \texttt{X-Agent-TrueTime-Window} $\langle t_{E,\text{earliest}}, t_{F,\text{latest}}, \epsilon_{\text{eff}}[, \epsilon_{\text{verifier}}] \rangle$, where $t_{F,\text{latest}}$ may be advanced beyond $t_{E,\text{latest}}$ by causal or per-key settlement floors in Eq.~\eqref{eq:t_f_latest} and the 4th field $\epsilon_{\text{verifier}}$ is included on settled mutations whenever $\epsilon_{\text{verifier}} \ne \epsilon_{\text{eff}}$ and defaults to $\epsilon_{\text{eff}}$ when omitted on single-node \texttt{IMMEDIATE} reads, so that downstream receivers deterministically verify $t_{F,\text{latest}} - t_{E,\text{earliest}} \ge 2\epsilon_{\text{verifier}} > 0$ on \texttt{RELEASED} frames and exact equality $t_{\text{now},i,\text{latest}} - t_{\text{now},i,\text{earliest}} = 2\epsilon_{\text{gateway}}$ on \texttt{IMMEDIATE} reads). The fundamental TrueTime invariant guarantees that at the instant of query on node $u$:
\begin{equation}
t_{\text{now},u,\text{earliest}} \le t_{\text{real}} \le t_{\text{now},u,\text{latest}}
\label{eq:truetime_invariant}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Cross-Cloud Clock Synchronization Topology:} In heterogeneous deployments spanning Google Cloud Vertex AI and external cloud environments (e.g., AWS Bedrock, Salesforce Agentforce, or SAP S/4HANA), Agent-Chronos supports two deployment modes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{(i) Federated Egress Gateway Mode:} External agent runtimes route both upstream epoch settlement signals and outbound MCP/A2A tool calls through a regional Google Cloud Apigee / Envoy Chronos gateway so that epoch commit timestamps $T_E$ and egress commit-wait checks $T_{\text{now},\text{gateway}}$ are anchored by the regional TrueTime cluster ($\epsilon_u \in [1, 7]\text{ ms}$). To prevent differential WAN transit jitter on the pre-gateway path from inverting external commit/abort signals prior to gateway arrival, either (a) external verifiers attach origin-signed PTP/NTP bounds $\mathcal{W}(T_{\text{origin}})$, in which case $\epsilon_{\text{eff}} = \lceil (\epsilon_{\text{origin}} + \epsilon_{\text{gateway}})/2 \rceil$ inherits the origin clock's uncertainty bound and---whenever abort-capable external verifiers communicate with the federated gateway across a WAN without a pre-minting quorum wait---the effective Phase~2 abort-propagation margin in Eq.~\eqref{eq:pacing_wait} incorporates the pre-gateway one-way WAN delay spread ($\Delta_{\text{abort\_prop}}^{\text{eff}} = \Delta_{\text{abort\_prop}} + \Delta_{\text{wan\_pre}}$, applied once in Phase~2 with zero pre-minting delay); or (b) the federated gateway requires a synchronous quorum ACK from participating verifiers of epoch $E$ before minting $T_E$ at its local TrueTime clock (using intra-cluster $\Delta_{\text{abort\_prop}}$ in Phase~2 without double-counting $\Delta_{\text{wan\_pre}}$). Under option~(b), when all abort-capable verifiers participate in a unanimous monotonic quorum (where no verifier can emit an abort after a positive ACK), unanimous ACK arrival alone precludes any racing abort with $\Delta_{\text{wan\_pre}} = 0\text{ ms}$ and unconditionally preserves the zero-byte wire invariant (this unanimous mode is mandatory for irreversible external endpoints); when a non-unanimous subset quorum or an asynchronous out-of-band guardrail can emit aborts independently of the quorum ACK channel, the gateway waits a conservative pre-minting jitter margin $\Delta_{\text{wan\_pre}}$ once before minting $T_E$ and assigns a monotonic quorum-epoch fence token, guaranteeing strict zero-byte pre-wire revocation as an operational scope condition under bounded pre-gateway network delay ($\le \Delta_{\text{wan\_pre}} + \Delta_{\text{wait}}$, where $\Delta_{\text{wait}}$ is defined in Section~\ref{sec:architecture}-C3, Eq.~\eqref{eq:pacing_wait}) while invoking deterministic post-release compensation as a fail-safe on reversible endpoints if a catastrophic WAN stall exceeds $\Delta_{\text{wan\_pre}} + \Delta_{\text{wait}}$.
    \item \textbf{(ii) Hardware PTP Sidecar Mode:} Non-Google cloud participants query local GPS/atomic Precision Time Protocol hardware clocks (e.g., AWS Time Sync Service \texttt{/dev/ptp0} with hardware-reported error bound $\epsilon_{\text{PTP},u} \in [1, 10]\text{ ms}$) at both verification and egress nodes and advertise $\epsilon_{\text{PTP},u}$ in $\mathcal{W}(T_{\text{now},u})$ (noting that when invoking unmodified third-party SaaS endpoints lacking an ingress sidecar across concurrent writers, distributed PTP sidecars still route through a single logical egress serialization gateway for that SaaS target).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Decoupled Swarm Causal Vectors and Per-Destination Sequence Coordinates}
While $\mathcal{O}(1)$-space Hybrid Logical Clocks (HLCs)~\cite{kulkarni2014hlc} track a single scalar logical-physical frontier, multi-agent swarms dynamically fork parallel sibling subagents whose speculative branches must be tested for strict causal precedence ($\mathcal{V}_1 \prec_{\mathcal{V}} \mathcal{V}_2$) versus concurrent incomparability ($\mathcal{V}_1 \parallel \mathcal{V}_2$). At the same time, using a single swarm-wide vector clock simultaneously as a per-destination CBCAST sequence counter would stall downstream ingress sidecars whenever an agent executes internal reasoning turns, dispatches \texttt{IMMEDIATE} reads, or routes unicast tool calls to distinct endpoints $d_1 \ne d_2$. Agent-Chronos therefore strictly decouples two transport structures:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Swarm-Wide Composite Causal Vector ($\mathcal{V}_i = \langle \mathbf{C}_i, t_{\text{max},i} \rangle$):} Maintained by each agent $a_i \in \text{AgentID}$, where $\mathbf{C}_i: \text{AgentID} \to \mathbb{N}_0$ is a Fidge/Mattern vector clock~\cite{fidge1988timestamps, mattern1988virtual} over active swarm agent IDs (with $\text{dom}(\mathbf{C}_i)$ denoting the finite non-zero support and missing keys defaulting to $0$) and $t_{\text{max},i} \in \mathbb{N}_0$ (initialized to $0$ and taking values in $\mathbb{N}^+$ after the first clock sample; equivalently written $\mathcal{V}_i.t_{\text{max}} \equiv t_{\text{max},i}$) is the maximum TrueTime upper bound ($t_{\text{latest}}$) observed across $a_i$'s causal history. Evaluated strictly over the logical vector component $\mathbf{C}$ (with $t_{\text{max},i}$ serving as a commit-wait floor and physical tie-breaker for concurrent writes), strict causal precedence over $D = \text{dom}(\mathbf{C}_1) \cup \text{dom}(\mathbf{C}_2)$ is defined as $\mathcal{V}_1 \prec_{\mathcal{V}} \mathcal{V}_2 \iff (\forall k \in D,\, \mathbf{C}_1[k] \le \mathbf{C}_2[k]) \land (\exists k \in D,\, \mathbf{C}_1[k] < \mathbf{C}_2[k])$, and concurrency as $\mathcal{V}_1 \parallel \mathcal{V}_2 \iff \neg(\mathcal{V}_1 \prec_{\mathcal{V}} \mathcal{V}_2) \land \neg(\mathcal{V}_2 \prec_{\mathcal{V}} \mathcal{V}_1)$.
    \item \textbf{Per-Destination Transport Sequence Coordinate ($\text{Seq}_{\text{dest}}[d, a_i]$):} For each destination endpoint $d$, the egress gateway maintains a dedicated gap-free monotonic counter $\text{Seq}_{\text{dest}}[d, a_i] \in \mathbb{N}_0$ (initialized to $0$ and incremented to $1, 2, \dots \in \mathbb{N}^+$ upon Phase~2 release) and a compact destination dependency map $\text{Dep}_{d,i}: \text{AgentID} \to \mathbb{N}_0$ (with missing keys defaulting to $0$) recording the highest destination-$d$ sequence numbers causally preceding the current frame.
\end{itemize}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Two-Stage Pre-Staging vs.\ Post-Phase-2 Sequence Assignment:} Parallel speculative branches ($\mathcal{V}_1 \parallel \mathcal{V}_2$) fork across distinct logical subagent identifiers $a_1 \ne a_2 \in \text{AgentID}$ (receiving independent vector entries $\mathbf{C}_1[a_1], \mathbf{C}_2[a_2]$). When a single agent $a_i \in \text{AgentID}$ initiates a mutating tool frame $F$ (\texttt{F.is\_mutation} $= \text{true}$) or an outbound A2A delegation turn at local TrueTime interval $T_{\text{now},i} = [t_{\text{now},i,\text{earliest}}, t_{\text{now},i,\text{latest}}]$, it constructs an isolated branch-local tentative pre-staging vector $\mathcal{V}_F = \langle \mathbf{C}_F, t_{\text{max},F} \rangle$ from its committed state $\langle \mathbf{C}_i, t_{\text{max},i} \rangle$ using an atomic per-agent tentative ticket counter $c_{\text{tent},i} \leftarrow c_{\text{tent},i} + 1$ (whereas exploratory read-only queries with \texttt{F.is\_mutation} $= \text{false}$ inspect $\langle \mathbf{C}_i, t_{\text{max},i} \rangle$ without incrementing $c_{\text{tent},i}$ or mutating $\mathbf{C}_i$, while advancing $t_{\text{max},i} \leftarrow \max(t_{\text{max},i}, t_{\text{now},i,\text{latest}}) \in \mathbb{N}^+$ upon sampling $T_{\text{now},i}$; furthermore, overlapping same-agent mutations or A2A delegations from $a_i$ with $c_{\text{tent},1} < c_{\text{tent},2}$ chain their causal settlement floor so $t_{F_2,\text{latest}} \ge t_{F_1,\text{latest}}$, ensuring ticket order $c_{\text{tent},1} < c_{\text{tent},2}$ and $(t_{F,\text{latest}}, c_{\text{tent}})$ Phase~2 release order in Section~\ref{sec:architecture}-C3 always coincide; if $F$ is revoked in Rule~4, $\mathcal{V}_F$ is discarded without mutating $a_i$'s shared committed state $\langle \mathbf{C}_i, t_{\text{max},i} \rangle$ and any child A2A branch spawned from $\mathcal{V}_F$ is transitively cascade-revoked via Rule~4 so no surviving downstream frame retains a revoked ticket $c_{\text{tent},i}$, while any numerical gap in committed $\mathbf{C}_i[a_i]$ after a later independent ticket commits is benign because vector precedence $\prec_{\mathcal{V}}$ tests $\le / <$ and gap-free ingress FIFO delivery relies exclusively on $\text{Seq}_{\text{dest}}[d, a_i]$):
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathbf{C}_F &\leftarrow \mathbf{C}_i[a_i \mapsto c_{\text{tent},i}], \\
    t_{\text{max},F} &\leftarrow \max(t_{\text{max},i},\, t_{\text{now},i,\text{latest}}).
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:vector_local}
    \end{equation}
    Crucially, the per-destination sequence counter $\text{Seq}_{\text{dest},F}[d, a_i] \leftarrow \text{Seq}_{\text{dest}}[d, a_i] + 1$ is assigned and incremented \textbf{strictly upon Phase 2 commit-wait release} of a mutating frame addressed to $d$, at which point $a_i$'s shared committed causal frontier atomically joins $\mathbf{C}_i \leftarrow \mathbf{C}_i \sqcup \mathbf{C}_F$ and $t_{\text{max},i} \leftarrow \max(t_{\text{max},i}, t_{F,\text{latest}})$ and records $\text{Seq}_{\text{dest},F}[d, a_i]$ in $\text{Dep}_{d,i}[a_i]$; meanwhile, the outbound frame $F$ itself serializes its settled dependency snapshot $\mathcal{V}_F = \langle \mathbf{C}_F, t_{F,\text{latest}} \rangle$ (where $t_{\text{max},F}$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:vector_local} is advanced to the settled commit upper bound $t_{F,\text{latest}}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:t_f_latest}) in \texttt{X-Agent-Causal-Fence-Vector} and its pre-staged $\text{Dep}_{d,F}$ (updated only with prior committed same-agent releases to $d$ and explicitly chained Rule~1/sibling writes) in \texttt{X-Agent-Dest-Seq}, preventing unrelated inbound responses arriving at $a_i$ during $F$'s Phase~1/Phase~2 hold from retroactively injecting false causal dependencies into $F$. Because $t_{\text{now},u} = t_{\text{real}} + \delta_u$ with $\delta_u \in [-\epsilon_u, +\epsilon_u]$ gives $t_{\text{now},u,\text{latest}} = t_{\text{real}} + \delta_u + \epsilon_u \le t_{\text{real}} + 2\epsilon_u$ (attained when node $u$'s clock runs fast at maximum drift $\delta_u = +\epsilon_u$), and because Phase~2 waits until $t_{\text{real}} \ge t_{F,\text{latest}}$ while unpaced local/A2A turns in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:vector_local}--\eqref{eq:vector_merge} take the pointwise maximum of existing $t_{\text{now},u,\text{latest}}$ bounds rather than summing $\epsilon_u$ across hops, the physical lead of $t_{\text{max},i}$ over absolute coordinate time $t_{\text{real}}$ is strictly non-accumulating across deep multi-agent delegation chains---satisfying $t_{\text{max},i} - t_{\text{real}} \le \max_u \epsilon_u$ at zero clock offset ($\delta_u = 0$) and the deterministic worst-case physical-time lead $t_{\text{max},i} - t_{\text{real}} \le \max(2\epsilon_{\text{src}} - d_{\min},\, 2\epsilon_i) \le 2\max_u \epsilon_u$ for ancestor uncertainty bound $\epsilon_{\text{src}} \triangleq \max_{u \in \text{Anc}(i)} \epsilon_u$ and minimum cumulative inter-node transit delay $d_{\min} > 0$ (and apparent lead $t_{\text{max},i} - t_{\text{now},i} \le \max(2\epsilon_{\text{src}} + \epsilon_i - d_{\min},\, \epsilon_i)$ relative to recipient $i$'s local clock reading $t_{\text{now},i} \ge t_{\text{real}} - \epsilon_i$) without inflating downstream $\Delta_{\text{floor}}$ (Section~\ref{sec:architecture}-C3, Eq.~\eqref{eq:pacing_wait}). To ensure transitive causal ordering across A2A delegations, any outbound A2A task delegation from $a_i$ to subagent $a_j$ that depends on an uncommitted write $F_1$ (targeting destination $d_1$) in epoch $E$ either awaits Phase~2 release of $F_1$ so $\text{Dep}_{d_1,i}$ carries the committed $\text{Seq}_{\text{dest},F_1}[d_1, a_i]$, or propagates a tentative epoch dependency handle $(E, F_1, \text{gw}(a_i))$ carrying origin gateway callback endpoint $\text{gw}(a_i)$ (so multi-hop descendants $a_i \to a_j \to a_k$ both subscribe directly to $\text{gw}(a_i)$ and receive relayed notifications along the delegation tree): $a_j$'s (and $a_k$'s) downstream calls $F_2$ (targeting $d_2$) inherit $E$'s Phase~1/Phase~2 barrier and are gated in Phase~2 until $\text{gw}(a_i)$ either delivers a signed release-commit token $\langle E, F_1, d_1, \text{Seq}_{\text{dest},F_1}[d_1, a_i], \mathcal{V}_{F_1} \rangle$---merging $\mathcal{V}_{F_1}$ into $\mathcal{V}_{F_2}$ for all $d_2$ and merging $\text{Seq}_{\text{dest},F_1}[d_1, a_i]$ into $\text{Dep}_{d_2,F_2}[a_i]$ if and only if $d_2 = d_1$ prior to releasing $F_2$---or delivers a signed negative revocation token $\langle E, F_1, \text{REVOKED} \rangle$ upon Rule~4 abort (or gateway lease-heartbeat expiry $\tau_{\text{lease}} + 2\epsilon_{\text{eff}}$), which immediately aborts $F_2$ with HTTP 409 Conflict without indefinite blocking. Because frames revoked pre-wire during Phase~1 or Phase~2 are purged before $\text{Seq}_{\text{dest}}[d, a_i]$ is incremented, pre-wire revocations never create sequence gaps and never transmit traffic toward downstream targets; payload-free tombstone sequence ACKs are emitted only if a post-release transport stream resets mid-flight on a sidecar-fronted link (and are suppressed for unmodified SaaS endpoints).
    \item \textbf{Causal Merge Rule and Hierarchical $>100$-Agent Compression:} When agent $a_j$ receives an A2A or tool response frame carrying $\mathcal{V}_i = \langle \mathbf{C}_i, t_{\text{max},i} \rangle$ and per-destination watermarks $\text{Dep}_{d,i}$, it merges $\mathcal{V}_i$ via the pointwise join operator ($\sqcup$) across $\text{dom}(\mathbf{C}_j) \cup \text{dom}(\mathbf{C}_i)$:
    \begin{equation}
    \mathcal{V}_j.\text{merge}(\mathcal{V}_i) = \left\langle \mathbf{C}_j \sqcup \mathbf{C}_i,\, \max(t_{\text{max}, j},\, t_{\text{max}, i}) \right\rangle,
    \label{eq:vector_merge}
    \end{equation}
    where $(\mathbf{C}_j \sqcup \mathbf{C}_i)[k] \triangleq \max(\mathbf{C}_j[k], \mathbf{C}_i[k])$, and merges $\text{Dep}_{d,j}[k] \leftarrow \max(\text{Dep}_{d,j}[k], \text{Dep}_{d,i}[k])$. Mapping active swarm agent IDs ($|\text{AgentID}| \le 16$) to a 16-element positional integer array in compact JSON \texttt{\{"c":[c\_0,\allowbreak ...,\allowbreak c\_15],\allowbreak "t":t\_max,\allowbreak "b":bitmap\}} (with per-session counters $c_k < 10^4$, a 19-digit nanosecond integer \texttt{t}, and a 16-bit integer bitmap $\texttt{b} \le 65535$ encoding active colliding sibling forks as defined in Section~\ref{sec:architecture}-D) requires $69\text{--}121\text{ bytes}$ of unformatted raw JSON ($117\text{--}121\text{ bytes}$ at 4-digit counters, or up to $142\text{--}184\text{ bytes}$ when including optional whitespace/epoch metadata, remaining $\le 190\text{ bytes}$) and $\le 256\text{ bytes}$ ($4\lceil 190/3 \rceil$) when Base64-encoded in HTTP/2 headers. For larger enterprise swarms ($>100$ agents), Agent-Chronos supports a two-tier hierarchical vector and sparse active-set compression profile: subagents are partitioned into $G \le 16$ domain clusters where an active writer $a_i \in g$ transitions to quiescent and is folded into its cluster summary watermark via $c_{\text{cluster}}[g] \leftarrow \max(c_{\text{cluster}}[g], \mathbf{C}[a_i])$ only after all of its released mutating frames have either been explicitly acknowledged by target Chronos ingress sidecars (or satisfied the observable local TrueTime bound $t_{\text{now},u,\text{earliest}} > t_{F,\text{latest}} + t_{\text{wan,max}} + \Delta_{\text{jitter\_hold}}$) and, for sidecar-free SaaS endpoints, completed synchronous HTTP 2xx execution (or terminal compensating saga resolution) across all in-flight pipeline locks. For any agent $a_k \in g$, its effective coordinate in $\mathcal{V}$ is evaluated as $\tilde{\mathbf{C}}[a_k] = \mathbf{C}_{\text{delta}}[a_k]$ when $a_k \in \text{dom}(\mathbf{C}_{\text{delta}})$ is in the sparse active delta map ($\le 16$ concurrent writers per destination key-space) and $\tilde{\mathbf{C}}[a_k] = c_{\text{cluster}}[g]$ once folded ($a_k \notin \text{dom}(\mathbf{C}_{\text{delta}})$), ensuring that pre-fold vs.\ post-fold comparisons ($\mathbf{C}_{\text{delta},1}[a_i] \le c_{\text{cluster},2}[g]$) preserve $\mathcal{V}_1 \prec_{\mathcal{V}} \mathcal{V}_2$ while concurrent writers remain unfolded in $\mathbf{C}_{\text{delta}}$ so $\mathcal{V}_1 \parallel \mathcal{V}_2$ is never collapsed (and Rule~1 in Section~\ref{sec:architecture}-C1 only inspects $\mathbf{C}_{\text{delta}}$ because folded agents have no unapplied writes). Binary-packing $c_{\text{cluster}}[1..16]$ ($16 \times 4 = 64\text{ B}$), up to $16$ active delta pairs $\langle a_k\text{ (uint16)}, \mathbf{C}_{\text{delta}}[a_k]\text{ (uint32)}\rangle$ ($16 \times 6 = 96\text{ B}$), int64 $t_{F,\text{latest}}$ ($8\text{ B}$), and the 16-bit cluster-collision bitmap $\texttt{b}$ ($2\text{ B}$) totals $170\text{ raw bytes} \le 192\text{ B}$ ($228\text{ Base64 bytes} \le 256\text{ B}$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Two-Phase L7 Speculative Staging and TrueTime Commit-Wait Pacing}
Because MCP \texttt{tools/call} and A2A task dispatches use HTTP \texttt{POST} for both queries and state mutations, the L7 gateway inspects the registered MCP tool annotation (\texttt{readOnlyHint: true|false}) or OpenAPI operation metadata to set \texttt{F.is\_mutation} $\in \{\text{true}, \text{false}\}$, extracts the composite target resource key-set $\mathcal{K}(F) \subseteq \mathcal{K}$ via registered per-tool JSONPath selectors over MCP \texttt{params.arguments}, A2A task/artifact payloads, or OpenAPI path/query/body parameters (e.g., $\{\texttt{\$.ledger\_id}\}$, multi-key $\{\texttt{\$.debit\_id}, \texttt{\$.credit\_id}\}$ for atomic transfers, or fallback universal wildcard $\{\texttt{*}\}$ for unmapped or range/aggregate queries, where $\{\texttt{*}\}$ intersects every non-empty key-set $\mathcal{K}(F')$ at destination $d$, $\{\texttt{*}\} \cap \mathcal{K}(F') = \mathcal{K}(F') \ne \emptyset$, consults both the destination-wide wildcard floor $t_{\text{key\_floor}}[d, \texttt{*}]$ and an $\mathcal{O}(1)$ running destination-wide summary watermark $t_{\text{max\_all\_keys}}[d] \triangleq \max_{k \in \mathcal{K}} t_{\text{key\_floor}}[d, k]$ maintained across all per-key floor updates at $d$, and maps to a destination-wide root lock in the sender-side SaaS pipeline lock table with hierarchical intention-lock compatibility---so specific-key operations acquire an intention lock [$\text{IS}/\text{IX}$] on $\{\texttt{*}\}$ ordered canonically before their specific resource key locks, while wildcard operations [$\mathcal{K}(F) = \{\texttt{*}\}$, or containing $\texttt{*}$] acquire an exclusive root lock on $\{\texttt{*}\}$ that gates and drains all active specific-key pipeline locks at $d$), and reads the upstream speculative epoch identifier $E$ and causal vector $\mathcal{V}_F$ from the request context. Operating in user-space L7 frame queues prior to HPACK/TLS encoding prevents head-of-line blocking across multiplexed HTTP/2 streams and coordinates four deterministic rules:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Conditional Safe Read-Only Bypass:} If \texttt{F.is\_mutation} $= \text{false}$ and neither (a) a causally preceding write $F_{\text{prior}}$ ($\mathcal{V}_{F_{\text{prior}}} \prec_{\mathcal{V}} \mathcal{V}_F$) across Phase~1 (\texttt{STAGE\_HELD}) or Phase~2 (\texttt{PACED\_HELD}) nor (b) a concurrent sibling write $F_{\text{sib}}$ currently in Phase~2 (\texttt{PACED\_HELD}) with an earlier or overlapping commit interval $t_{F_{\text{sib}},\text{earliest}} \equiv t_{E_{\text{sib}},\text{earliest}} \le t_{\text{now},F,\text{latest}}$ (evaluated in $\mathcal{O}(1)$ time for wildcard reads $\mathcal{K}(F) = \{\texttt{*}\}$ via a destination-level active \texttt{STAGE\_HELD}/\texttt{PACED\_HELD} write summary mirroring the $\{\texttt{*}\}$ intention-lock root, and on unmodified external SaaS endpoints lacking an ingress sidecar, requiring no overlapping in-flight write holding a sender-side per-resource SaaS pipeline lock on $\mathcal{K}(F)$) shares an overlapping resource key ($\mathcal{K}(F_{\text{prior/sib}}) \cap \mathcal{K}(F) \ne \emptyset$), pacing status is set to \texttt{IMMEDIATE} and the frame is dispatched with $0.00\text{ ms}$ pacing delay. To ensure unsettled speculative sibling writes in Phase~1 (\texttt{STAGE\_HELD}) linearize strictly after concurrent exploratory \texttt{IMMEDIATE} reads without stalling reads for $200\text{--}5{,}000\text{ ms}$, dispatching an \texttt{IMMEDIATE} read $F$ advances the monotonic per-key floor $t_{\text{key\_floor}}[d, k] \leftarrow \max(t_{\text{key\_floor}}[d, k], t_{\text{now},F,\text{latest}} + 1\text{ ns})$ for all $k \in \mathcal{K}(F)$ (including $t_{\text{key\_floor}}[d, \texttt{*}]$ when $\texttt{*} \in \mathcal{K}(F)$, which also checks any active specific-key $F_{\text{sib}} \in \texttt{PACED\_HELD}$ at $d$) as well as the $\mathcal{O}(1)$ destination summary watermark $t_{\text{max\_all\_keys}}[d] \leftarrow \max(t_{\text{max\_all\_keys}}[d], t_{\text{now},F,\text{latest}} + 1\text{ ns})$ (while Phase~2 visibility is evaluated over the egress gateway's locally observed settlement state, replicated across distributed PTP sidecars via intra-cluster settlement tokens and read-floor gossip within $\Delta_{\text{abort\_prop}}$---dynamically advancing $t_{F_{\text{sib}},\text{latest}} \leftarrow \max(t_{F_{\text{sib}},\text{latest}}, t_{\text{now},F,\text{latest}} + 1\text{ ns})$, cascading that floor advancement to any later-ticketed frames from the same agent in \texttt{PACED\_HELD} to preserve $(t_{F,\text{latest}}, c_{\text{tent}})$ order, and extending $F_{\text{sib}}$'s Phase~2 hold \emph{at most once per cross-sidecar gossip window} $\Delta_{\text{abort\_prop}}$ if an overlapping cross-sidecar \texttt{IMMEDIATE} read floor dispatched prior to $F_{\text{sib}}$'s settlement token replication arrives while $F_{\text{sib}}$ is in \texttt{PACED\_HELD}, preventing write starvation because any subsequent reads arriving after $F_{\text{sib}}$'s settlement token is replicated observe $F_{\text{sib}} \in \texttt{PACED\_HELD}$ under condition~(b) and enter \texttt{SAFE\_READ\_WAIT} rather than dispatching as \texttt{IMMEDIATE}, or evaluated deterministically with zero gossip lag when same-destination calls route through a consistent per-destination logical gateway shard). Otherwise, $F$ enters \texttt{SAFE\_READ\_WAIT} and is held at the egress gateway until the blocking write ($F_{\text{prior}}$ or $F_{\text{sib}}$) transitions to \texttt{RELEASED} and assigns its sequence coordinate $\text{Seq}_{\text{dest},F_{\text{prior/sib}}}[d, a_{\text{prior/sib}}]$, which is merged into $\text{Dep}_{d,F}$ prior to dispatching $F$. If a causally preceding write $F_{\text{prior}}$ (case~a) is revoked via Rule~4, the dependent read $F$ is aborted with HTTP 409 Conflict; conversely, if a concurrent sibling write $F_{\text{sib}}$ (case~b, $\mathcal{V}_{F_{\text{sib}}} \parallel \mathcal{V}_F$) is revoked pre-wire during Phase~2, $F_{\text{sib}}$ never takes effect and $F$ is immediately unblocked and re-evaluated against all remaining active overlapping writes under Rule~1 (remaining in \texttt{SAFE\_READ\_WAIT} if another overlapping $F_{\text{prior}}$ or $F'_{\text{sib}}$ is still active, or dispatching as \texttt{IMMEDIATE} once no blocking write remains). Furthermore, on sidecar-fronted links, the egress gateway merges the sequence coordinate $\text{Seq}_{\text{dest},F_{\text{sib}}}[d, a_{\text{sib}}]$ of any overlapping chained or recently released sibling write (within $\Delta_{\text{jitter\_hold}}$) into $\text{Dep}_{d,F}$, and any read $F$ with non-empty $\text{Dep}_{d,F}$ attaches $\langle \text{Seq}_{\text{dest},F}=0, \text{Dep}_{d,F} \rangle$ in \texttt{X-Agent-Dest-Seq} so the ingress sidecar holds $F$ until $\forall a_k \in \text{dom}(\text{Dep}_{d,F}),\, \text{Seq}_{\text{loc},d}[a_k] \ge \text{Dep}_{d,F}[a_k]$, preventing WAN jitter from reordering a post-release read ahead of an in-flight released write.
    \item \textbf{Phase 1 --- Speculative Epoch Staging ($\Delta_{\text{stage}}$):} If \texttt{F.is\_mutation} $= \text{true}$ and $F$ depends on an active upstream speculative reasoning or policy-verification branch $E$ (duration $200\text{--}5{,}000\text{ ms}$), the L7 filter holds $F$ in user-space staging memory (\texttt{STAGE\_HELD}) while upstream generation and parallel policy verification execute concurrently. Once upstream epoch $E$ completes verification and commits its decision at verifier TrueTime interval $T_E = [t_{E,\text{earliest}}, t_{E,\text{latest}}]$ (with verifier uncertainty $\epsilon_{\text{verifier}}$), the epoch broadcasts an intra-cluster settlement token $\mathcal{W}(T_E)$ containing $t_{E,\text{latest}}^* = \max(t_{E,\text{latest}}, t_{\text{max},F})$, and the gateway computes the frame's monotonic commit upper bound $t_{F,\text{latest}}$ in $\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{K}(F)|)$ time by incorporating the per-key settlement floor $t_{\text{key\_floor}}[d, k] \in \mathbb{N}_0$ across all $k \in \mathcal{K}(F)$, the destination-wide wildcard floor $t_{\text{key\_floor}}[d, \texttt{*}]$, and---whenever $\texttt{*} \in \mathcal{K}(F)$---the $\mathcal{O}(1)$ running destination summary watermark $t_{\text{max\_all\_keys}}[d]$ across all tracked keys at $d$:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:t_f_latest}
    \resizebox{0.87\columnwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle
    \begin{aligned}
    t_{F,\text{latest}} = \max\Big(&t_{E,\text{latest}}^*,\, t_{\text{key\_floor}}[d, \texttt{*}], \\
    &\max_{k \in \mathcal{K}(F)} t_{\text{key\_floor}}[d, k],\, \mathbb{I}(\texttt{*} \in \mathcal{K}(F)) \cdot t_{\text{max\_all\_keys}}[d]\Big),
    \end{aligned}
    $}
    \end{equation}
    immediately advancing $t_{\text{key\_floor}}[d, k] \leftarrow \max(t_{\text{key\_floor}}[d, k], t_{F,\text{latest}} + 1\text{ ns})$ for all $k \in \mathcal{K}(F)$ as well as $t_{\text{max\_all\_keys}}[d] \leftarrow \max(t_{\text{max\_all\_keys}}[d], t_{F,\text{latest}} + 1\text{ ns})$ upon Phase~1 settlement (and replicating the updated floor across distributed PTP sidecars in the settlement token while enforcing a minimum elapsed Phase~2 hold of at least $\Delta_{\text{abort\_prop}}$ after local settlement token broadcast so that cross-sidecar replication lag and any dynamic Phase~2 read-floor updates complete before the sibling bitmap $\texttt{b}$ is finalized at Phase~2 release).
    \item \textbf{Phase 2 --- TrueTime Commit-Wait Serialization ($\Delta_{\text{wait}}$):} Upon receiving the settlement token for epoch $E$, the L7 egress pacer transitions $F$ to \texttt{PACED\_HELD} and halts wire transmission until its local TrueTime lower bound $t_{\text{now},\text{earliest}} = t_{\text{now},\text{gateway}} - \epsilon_{\text{gateway}}$ strictly surpasses $t_{F,\text{latest}} + \Delta_{\text{abort\_prop}}$ (enforcing monotonic same-agent Phase~2 release in $(t_{F,\text{latest}}, c_{\text{tent}})$ order so an earlier-staged frame from $a_s$ is never overtaken in $\text{Seq}_{\text{dest}}[d, a_s]$ assignment by a later-staged frame from $a_s$), where $\Delta_{\text{abort\_prop}}$ is the upper bound on intra-cluster control-plane abort propagation delay from any sibling verification node ($\Delta_{\text{abort\_prop}} \le 0.5\text{ ms}$ over intra-cluster gRPC, or $0\text{ ms}$ under co-located quorum settlement):
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:pacing_wait}
    \resizebox{0.87\columnwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle
    \begin{aligned}
    \Delta_{\text{wait}} &= \max\!\left(0,\, t_{F,\text{latest}} + \Delta_{\text{abort\_prop}} - t_{\text{now},\text{earliest}}\right), \\
    \mathbb{E}[\Delta_{\text{wait}}] &\approx \max\!\left(0,\, \mathbb{E}[\Delta_{\text{floor}}] + 2\epsilon_{\text{eff}} + \Delta_{\text{abort\_prop}} - \bar{\Delta}_{\text{ipc}}\right)
    \end{aligned}
    $}
    \end{equation}
    where the first line is the \textbf{normative runtime barrier} evaluated by polling local TrueTime hardware (since under signed local clock offsets $\delta_u \in [-\epsilon_u, +\epsilon_u]$, physical IPC transit $\Delta_{\text{real\_ipc}} \triangleq t_{\text{real,now}} - t_{\text{real},E}$, and combined causal/key floor $\Delta_{\text{floor}} = t_{F,\text{latest}} - t_{E,\text{latest}} \ge 0$, the instantaneous wait equals $\max(0, \Delta_{\text{floor}} + 2\epsilon_{\text{eff}} + (\delta_{\text{verifier}} - \delta_{\text{gateway}}) + \Delta_{\text{abort\_prop}} - \Delta_{\text{real\_ipc}})$), and the second line gives the tight Jensen's inequality approximation ($\mathbb{E}[\max(0, X)] \approx \max(0, \mathbb{E}[X])$, exact to $<0.4\%$ zero-clamping) under zero-mean clock offsets ($\mathbb{E}[\delta_{\text{verifier}} - \delta_{\text{gateway}}] = 0$), reducing to $\max(0, 2\epsilon_{\text{eff}} + \Delta_{\text{abort\_prop}} - \bar{\Delta}_{\text{ipc}})$ whenever $\Delta_{\text{floor}} = 0$, where $\bar{\Delta}_{\text{ipc}} \triangleq \mathbb{E}[\Delta_{\text{real\_ipc}}] = \mathbb{E}[t_{\text{now},\text{gateway}} - t_E]$ denotes expected IPC transit relative to the raw settlement midpoint $t_E \triangleq t_{E,\text{latest}} - \epsilon_{\text{verifier}}$ (which coincides with the floor-adjusted midpoint $t_F^* \triangleq t_{F,\text{latest}} - \epsilon_{\text{verifier}}$ when $\Delta_{\text{floor}} = 0$). A causal or policy abort emitted by a sibling verifier at TrueTime interval $T_{\text{abort}} = [t_{\text{abort,earliest}}, t_{\text{abort,latest}}]$ that either explicitly targets epoch $E$ or preempts a conflicting resource key $k \in \mathcal{K}(F)$ whose physical emission satisfies $t_{\text{real,abort}} \le t_{F,\text{latest}}$ (which implies the conservative interval-overlap condition $t_{\text{abort,earliest}} \le t_{F,\text{latest}}$ since $t_{\text{abort,earliest}} \le t_{\text{real,abort}}$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:truetime_invariant}) is deterministically guaranteed to arrive before release: waiting until $t_{\text{now},\text{earliest}} \ge t_{F,\text{latest}} + \Delta_{\text{abort\_prop}}$ ensures by Eq.~\eqref{eq:truetime_invariant} that $t_{\text{real}} \ge t_{F,\text{latest}} + \Delta_{\text{abort\_prop}} \ge t_{\text{real,abort}} + \Delta_{\text{abort\_prop}}$, so any abort physically emitted at $t_{\text{real,abort}} \le t_{F,\text{latest}}$ has both been emitted and physically arrived at the egress pacer before $F$ is released (whereas aborts physically emitted slightly later in $(t_{F,\text{latest}}, t_{F,\text{latest}} + 2\epsilon_{\text{abort}}]$ that still satisfy $t_{\text{abort,earliest}} \le t_{F,\text{latest}}$ are intercepted opportunistically if they arrive prior to $\Delta_{\text{wait}}$ expiration, or deterministically if the barrier adds $+2\epsilon_{\text{abort}}$).
    \item \textbf{Pre-Wire In-Flight Revocation:} If epoch $E$ aborts during Phase 1 ($\Delta_{\text{stage}}$) or if a cross-node policy abort targeting $E$ or conflicting on $\mathcal{K}(F)$ with $t_{\text{abort,earliest}} \le t_{F,\text{latest}}$ arrives within the Phase 2 commit-wait window ($\Delta_{\text{wait}}$), the unencoded frame $F$ is immediately purged from the user-space L7 queue before any bytes enter the HPACK encoder, TLS record layer, or kernel TCP socket buffer. The gateway returns a deterministic HTTP 409 Conflict response to the calling agent, ensuring \textbf{zero mutating payload bytes} reach the downstream SaaS endpoint.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Ingress Causal Re-Sequencing Barrier Against WAN Jitter}
While sender-side commit-wait pacing orders departure timestamps in $t_{\text{real}}$, public WAN transit jitter ($\pm 10\text{ ms}$ to $60\text{ ms}$) can reorder packets in flight. For endpoints fronted by an Agent-Chronos ingress sidecar or enterprise API gateway (e.g., fronting SAP S/4HANA OData/RFC connectors), the ingress filter maintains per-destination applied sequence watermarks $\text{Seq}_{\text{loc},d}$ and buffers any arriving mutating frame $F$ from sender $a_s$ until $\text{Seq}_{\text{loc},d}[a_s] = \text{Seq}_{\text{dest},F}[d, a_s] - 1$ and $\forall a_k \in \text{dom}(\text{Dep}_{d,F}),\, \text{Seq}_{\text{loc},d}[a_k] \ge \text{Dep}_{d,F}[a_k]$ (and similarly holds any dependent read carrying $\text{Dep}_{d,F}$ until $\forall a_k \in \text{dom}(\text{Dep}_{d,F}),\, \text{Seq}_{\text{loc},d}[a_k] \ge \text{Dep}_{d,F}[a_k]$; note that because $\text{Seq}_{\text{dest}}[d, a_s]$ is scoped per agent $a_s$ and each agent $a_s$ attaches to a single co-located local PTP sidecar---or shares a consistent logical gateway shard---different agents $a_{s1} \ne a_{s2}$ on distinct PTP sidecars maintain independent sequence counters $\text{Seq}_{\text{dest}}[d, a_{s1}]$ and $\text{Seq}_{\text{dest}}[d, a_{s2}]$ without cross-sidecar sequence contention). For concurrent sibling writes ($\mathcal{V}_1 \parallel \mathcal{V}_2$) where $F_1$ (from $a_{s1}$) precedes $F_2$ (from $a_{s2}$) in deterministic $(t_{F,\text{latest}},\, \text{AgentID})$ order ($t_{F_1,\text{latest}} < t_{F_2,\text{latest}}$, or equality broken by $\text{AgentID}(a_{s1}) < \text{AgentID}(a_{s2})$), the monotonic per-key settlement floor $t_{\text{key\_floor}}[d, k]$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:t_f_latest} guarantees that any sibling settling Phase~1 after $F_1$ receives $t_{F_2,\text{latest}} > t_{F_1,\text{latest}}$. Upon Phase~2 release of the later-ordered write $F_2$, the egress gateway finalizes the 16-bit sibling fork bitmap \texttt{b} in \texttt{X-Agent-Causal-Fence-Vector}, setting bits \emph{strictly} for concurrent sibling agents $a_{s1}$ that have already settled Phase~1 with an earlier $(t_{F_1,\text{latest}},\, \text{AgentID})$ order and overlapping resource keys ($\mathcal{K}(F_1) \cap \mathcal{K}(F_2) \ne \emptyset$) targeting $d$. Because monotonic same-agent Phase~2 release guarantees that any multiple overlapping writes from $a_{s1}$ release in ticket order, $F_2$ only needs to reference the highest preceding overlapping ticket $c_{\text{tent},1}$ per flagged sibling $a_{s1}$, serializing the per-sibling coordinate map $\text{SibCoord}_{d,F_2}: a_{s1} \mapsto \langle t_{F_1,\text{latest}}, c_{\text{tent},1}, s_{\text{tomb}} \rangle$ in \texttt{X-Agent-Dest-Seq} with primary matching key $\langle a_{s1}, c_{\text{tent},1} \rangle$ (where $s_{\text{tomb}} = \text{Seq}_{\text{dest},F_1}[d, a_{s1}] > 0$ when $F_1$ has already completed Phase~2 release prior to $F_2$; whereas if $F_1$ is still in \texttt{PACED\_HELD} on a peer PTP sidecar so $s_{\text{tomb}} = 0$ at $F_2$'s departure, $a_{s1}$'s sidecar detects $F_2$'s overlapping settlement token via intra-cluster gossip, marks $F_1$ as watched, and emits a lightweight control-plane \texttt{SIBLING\_SEQ\_BIND} frame carrying $\langle a_{s1}, t_{F_1,\text{latest}}, c_{\text{tent},1}, s_{\text{tomb}} \rangle$ directly to destination $d$'s ingress sidecar immediately upon $F_1$'s Phase~2 release, or emits \texttt{SIBLING\_CLEAR} carrying $\langle a_{s1}, t_{F_1,\text{latest}}, c_{\text{tent},1} \rangle$ if $F_1$ is revoked during Phase~2; the ingress sidecar caches both in a bounded TTL set [$2\Delta_{\text{jitter\_hold}}$, i.e., $120\text{ ms}$ at $\Delta_{\text{jitter\_hold}} = 60\text{ ms}$] keyed by $\langle a_{s1}, c_{\text{tent},1} \rangle$ so that even if WAN jitter delivers \texttt{SIBLING\_CLEAR} or \texttt{SIBLING\_SEQ\_BIND} \emph{before} $F_2$ arrives, $F_2$ immediately recognizes $F_1$'s bound sequence or cleared state, while aborted, later-settled, read-only, or disjoint-key sibling branches never set bits in $F_2$'s bitmap). Upon ingress arrival of $F_2$ once $t_{\text{now},\text{earliest}} \ge t_{F_2,\text{latest}}$, the ingress sidecar initiates a bounded WAN jitter countdown $\Delta_{\text{jitter\_hold}}$ \emph{if and only if} $F_2$'s bitmap \texttt{b} contains at least one bit for an earlier-ordered sibling write $F_1$ that has neither been applied at the ingress sidecar nor recorded in the \texttt{SIBLING\_CLEAR} TTL cache; this hold terminates \textbf{immediately} ($0\text{ ms}$ further wait) as soon as all flagged earlier sibling writes $F_1$ arrive and are applied (or cleared), waiting the full $\Delta_{\text{jitter\_hold}} = (t_{\text{wan,max}} - t_{\text{wan,min}}) + 2\epsilon_{\text{gateway\_diff}} + \Delta_{\text{abort\_prop}}$ (where $\epsilon_{\text{gateway\_diff}} \triangleq \epsilon_{\text{gateway},1} \le \epsilon_{\text{PTP,max}}$ bounds the worst-case physical Phase~2 departure skew when peer sidecar~1 runs at $-\epsilon_{\text{gateway},1}$ while sidecar~2 runs at $+\epsilon_{\text{gateway},2}$, and $\epsilon_{\text{gateway\_diff}} = 0$ when $F_1$ and $F_2$ share the same physical gateway clock reference $\delta_1 = \delta_2$, reducing $\Delta_{\text{jitter\_hold}}$ to $t_{\text{wan,max}} - t_{\text{wan,min}} = 60\text{ ms}$ under a shared regional egress gateway) only when $F_1$ is pathologically delayed. Conflicting writes are applied in deterministic $(t_{F,\text{latest}},\, \text{AgentID})$ order once $t_{\text{now},\text{earliest}} \ge t_{F,\text{latest}}$. If a pathologically delayed earlier sibling write $F_1$ that was released from Phase~2 ($s_{\text{tomb}} = \text{Seq}_{\text{dest},F_1}[d, a_{s1}] > 0$, provided either in $F_2$ or via \texttt{SIBLING\_SEQ\_BIND}) fails to arrive before $F_2$'s $\Delta_{\text{jitter\_hold}}$ expires and $F_2$ is applied with $t_{F_1,\text{latest}} < t_{F_2,\text{latest}}$, the ingress sidecar proactively advances $\text{Seq}_{\text{loc},d}[a_{s1}]$ to $s_{\text{tomb}}$ upon $\Delta_{\text{jitter\_hold}}$ expiration while recording $\langle a_{s1}, t_{F_1,\text{latest}}, c_{\text{tent},1}, s_{\text{tomb}} \rangle$ in a late-rejection tombstone set (so subsequent disjoint-key frames from $a_{s1}$ with $\text{Seq}_{\text{dest}} = s_{\text{tomb}} + 1$ never stall between $60\text{ ms}$ and $180\text{ ms}$, and when the delayed $F_1$ eventually arrives it is deterministically rejected with HTTP 409 Stale Sibling Timestamp; if $s_{\text{tomb}} = 0$ at $\Delta_{\text{jitter\_hold}}$ expiration because neither $F_1$ nor \texttt{SIBLING\_SEQ\_BIND} has arrived, the ingress sidecar records the unbound tombstone $\langle a_{s1}, t_{F_1,\text{latest}}, c_{\text{tent},1} \rangle$ and emits a control-plane \texttt{TOMBSTONE\_FENCE} $\langle d, a_{s1}, c_{\text{tent},1} \rangle$ notification to $a_{s1}$'s egress sidecar, which fences $F_1$ and replies with a \texttt{TOMBSTONE\_ACK} $\langle d, a_{s1}, c_{\text{tent},1}, s_{\text{tomb}} \rangle$ frame binding $s_{\text{tomb}}$---also emitted automatically if a released HTTP/2 stream resets mid-flight via \texttt{RST\_STREAM}---so the ingress sidecar immediately skips $s_{\text{tomb}}$ in $\text{Seq}_{\text{loc},d}[a_{s1}]$). Each agent initializes $\text{AckTomb}_{d,i}: \text{AgentID} \to \mathbb{N}_0$ to $0$ and advances $\text{AckTomb}_{d,i}[a_{s1}] \leftarrow \max(\text{AckTomb}_{d,i}[a_{s1}], s_{\text{tomb}})$ upon processing a 409 tombstone rejection or \texttt{TOMBSTONE\_FENCE}. Upon tombstoning $s_{\text{tomb}} > 0$, the ingress sidecar cascades HTTP 409 aborts strictly to \emph{causally dependent} unapplied in-flight frames ($\mathcal{V}_{F_1} \prec_{\mathcal{V}} \mathcal{V}_F$, where $a_{s1}$ scopes $\mathcal{V}_{F_1}$ to $\mathcal{K}(F_1)$ so concurrent disjoint-key frames $F'_1$ with $\mathcal{K}(F'_1) \cap \mathcal{K}(F_1) = \emptyset$ satisfy $\mathcal{V}_{F_1} \nprec_{\mathcal{V}} \mathcal{V}_{F'_1}$ and are never cascade-aborted) carrying $\text{Dep}_{d,F}[a_{s1}] \ge s_{\text{tomb}}$ whose acknowledged-tombstone watermark in \texttt{X-Agent-Dest-Seq} satisfies $\text{AckTomb}_{d,F}[a_{s1}] < s_{\text{tomb}}$, while unblocking concurrent sibling reads ($\mathcal{V}_{F_1} \parallel \mathcal{V}_F$) once $\text{Seq}_{\text{loc},d}[a_{s1}] \ge s_{\text{tomb}}$ so they read the state committed by $F_2$ (and permitting post-409 recovery writes synthesized after the agent processes the rejection, which carry $\text{AckTomb}_{d,F}[a_{s1}] \ge s_{\text{tomb}}$). For unmodified external third-party SaaS APIs without an ingress sidecar, the sender-side gateway acquires per-resource pipeline locks in $\mathcal{K}(F)$ (with intention-lock $\text{IS}/\text{IX}$ vs.\ exclusive root-lock compatibility on $\{\texttt{*}\}$ as specified in Section~\ref{sec:architecture}-C) upon Phase~2 transition to \texttt{RELEASED} in deterministic $(t_{F,\text{latest}},\, \text{AgentID})$ order (and across multi-key sets in canonical lexicographical key order to prevent deadlocks), holds any dependent write or read $F_2$ ($\mathcal{K}(F_1) \cap \mathcal{K}(F_2) \ne \emptyset$) until the HTTP 2xx acknowledgment of $F_1$ returns, and pairs post-transmission HTTP 5xx faults with compensating sagas~\cite{garcia1987sagas}. To tolerate node failures of the shared logical egress serialization gateway during in-flight SaaS transmission or saga recovery, per-resource lock leases and pre-dispatch compensating saga intent records $\langle d, \mathcal{K}(F_1), \text{IdempKey}(F_1), \text{CompSpec}(F_1), \text{epoch}_{\text{fence}} \rangle$ are synchronously replicated to an intra-cluster Raft/Spanner-backed lock-lease table prior to wire dispatch, while the primary egress shard enforces a local kernel socket send/ACK timeout $\texttt{SO\_SNDTIMEO} = \texttt{TCP\_USER\_TIMEOUT} = 150\text{ ms} < \tau_{\text{lease}} - 2\epsilon_{\text{eff}}$ and verifies remaining TrueTime lease validity $t_{\text{lease\_exp}} - t_{\text{now,latest}} > \texttt{SO\_SNDTIMEO}$ before writing to the socket (arming an abortive \texttt{SO\_LINGER} \texttt{\{l\_onoff=1, l\_linger=0\}} \texttt{TCP\_RST} close and cloud SDN/VIP source-packet fence if the lease expires prior to HTTP 2xx completion so unsent kernel \texttt{sk\_buff} segments cannot drain after lease loss): if the primary egress gateway shard crashes or its lease expires ($\tau_{\text{lease}} = 250\text{ ms}$), the standby egress shard waits $\tau_{\text{lease}} + 2\epsilon_{\text{eff}}$ (accounting for worst-case cross-node clock uncertainty), increments the monotonic fencing epoch $\text{epoch}_{\text{fence}} \leftarrow \text{epoch}_{\text{fence}} + 1$, fence-isolates the failed gateway, resolves $\text{IdempKey}(F_1)$ on the external SaaS API (either invoking a provider cancellation/void endpoint or replaying $F_1$ under the identical $\text{IdempKey}(F_1)$ to deterministically settle $F_1$'s state and prevent any late-draining packet from executing uncompensated if not already committed), and drives $\text{CompSpec}(F_1)$ rollback to terminal completion before releasing the resource lock to queued frame $F_2$.

\section{Wire Protocol and Observability Invariants}
\label{sec:wire_protocol}
Internal pre-wire gateway queue states (\texttt{STAGE\_HELD}, \texttt{PACED\_HELD}) resolve to four terminal wire dispositions (\{\texttt{IMMEDIATE}, \texttt{SAFE\_READ\_WAIT}, \texttt{RELEASED}, \texttt{REVOKED}\}) prior to wire emission (while sidecar-to-sidecar control notifications for Phase~2 sibling revocations, sequence binding, and post-release stream resets use zero-payload HTTP/2 \texttt{HEADERS}-only control frames with \texttt{END\_STREAM=1} and \texttt{X-Agent-Pacing-Status} set to \texttt{SIBLING\_CLEAR}, \texttt{SIBLING\_SEQ\_BIND}, \texttt{TOMBSTONE\_FENCE}, or \texttt{TOMBSTONE\_ACK}, which are always dispatched on dedicated ephemeral HTTP/2 control stream IDs multiplexed over the \texttt{h2} connection---never on an active bidirectional MCP/A2A stream ID---and are terminated and consumed strictly at the Chronos sidecar without forwarding any request to the backend ERP/SaaS service). Each speculative unary or client-streaming gRPC/MCP mutation either opens a dedicated multiplexed HTTP/2 stream whose initial \texttt{HEADERS} and request \texttt{DATA} frames are held in user-space until Phase~2 completes, or---on long-lived bidirectional MCP/A2A streams---holds individual JSON-RPC request \texttt{DATA} frames in user-space \texttt{STAGE\_HELD}/\texttt{PACED\_HELD} queues prior to wire emission (carrying per-call \texttt{X-Agent-*} settlement fields inside the L7 JSON-RPC \texttt{\_meta} envelope upon Phase~2 release, which the Chronos ingress sidecar's L7 JSON-RPC framer inspects and strips/verifies per message identically to unary HTTP/2 headers before forwarding to the local MCP/A2A server, while session-closing HTTP/2 \texttt{END\_STREAM} trailers emit a cumulative session audit digest), strictly preserving the zero-byte wire invariant upon revocation without prematurely half-closing active bidirectional streams. Agent-Chronos injects five \texttt{X-Agent-*} settlement, pacing, and sequence HTTP/2 headers:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{X-Agent-TrueTime-Window}: Serialized settlement tuple $\langle t_{E,\text{earliest}}, t_{F,\text{latest}}, \epsilon_{\text{eff}}[, \epsilon_{\text{verifier}}] \rangle$ in integer nanoseconds for settled frames (including $\epsilon_{\text{verifier}}$ whenever $\epsilon_{\text{verifier}} \ne \epsilon_{\text{eff}}$, and defaulting to $\epsilon_{\text{eff}}$ on single-node \texttt{IMMEDIATE} reads), where $t_{F,\text{latest}}$ is given by Eq.~\eqref{eq:t_f_latest}, $\epsilon_{\text{eff}} = \lceil (\epsilon_{\text{verifier}} + \epsilon_{\text{gateway}})/2 \rceil$, and receivers verify $t_{F,\text{latest}} - t_{E,\text{earliest}} \ge 2\epsilon_{\text{verifier}} > 0$ on \texttt{RELEASED} frames (and exact equality $t_{\text{now},i,\text{latest}} - t_{\text{now},i,\text{earliest}} = 2\epsilon_{\text{gateway}}$ on \texttt{IMMEDIATE} reads).
    \item \texttt{X-Agent-Causal-Fence-Vector}: Compact base64/JSON serialization ($\le 256\text{ B}$) of settled $\mathcal{V}_F = \langle \mathbf{C}_F, t_{F,\text{latest}} \rangle$ and 16-bit active preceding sibling fork bitmap \texttt{b}.
    \item \texttt{X-Agent-Pacing-Status}: Terminal disposition in \{\texttt{IMMEDIATE}, \texttt{SAFE\_READ\_WAIT}, \texttt{RELEASED}, \texttt{REVOKED}\} (plus sidecar-internal control dispositions \texttt{SIBLING\_CLEAR}, \texttt{SIBLING\_SEQ\_BIND}, \texttt{TOMBSTONE\_FENCE}, \texttt{TOMBSTONE\_ACK}).
    \item \texttt{X-Agent-Pacing-Delay-Ms}: Post-settlement gateway hold duration in milliseconds ($\Delta_{\text{wait}}$ for \texttt{RELEASED} mutations, chained wait for \texttt{SAFE\_READ\_WAIT} reads, and elapsed Phase 2 hold prior to abort for \texttt{REVOKED} frames; Phase 1 $\Delta_{\text{stage}}$ is exported in OpenTelemetry attribute \texttt{chronos.stage\_delay\_ms}).
    \item \texttt{X-Agent-Dest-Seq}: Per-destination transport sequence coordinate and dependency tuple $\langle \text{Seq}_{\text{dest},F}[d, a_s], \text{Dep}_{d,F}, \text{AckTomb}_{d,F}, \text{SibCoord}_{d,F} \rangle$ attached to mutating calls and dependent reads (with $\text{Seq}_{\text{dest},F} = 0$ on dependent reads) for gap-free ingress FIFO delivery, sibling tombstone binding, and cascade-abort scoping.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Dual-Plane Observability of Revoked Frames:} When a frame is purged via Pre-Wire In-Flight Revocation, zero mutating bytes are transmitted to the external SaaS target. Instead, \texttt{X-Agent-Pacing-Status: REVOKED} and \texttt{X-Agent-Pacing-Delay-Ms} are emitted on (i) the synchronous HTTP 409 response wire back to the upstream orchestrator and (ii) a payload-free signed telemetry trailer exported to the cross-organization OpenTelemetry audit sink, ensuring $100\%$ protocol auditability without leaking aborted payloads to third-party APIs.

\subsection{Experimental Testbed and Workload Methodology}
We evaluated Agent-Chronos on an emulated cross-cloud HTTP/2 Envoy + FastMCP testbed modeling multi-agent financial ledger and procurement workflows (adapted from $\tau$-bench~\cite{yao2024taubench} tool-invocation schemas) between Google Cloud Vertex AI and an SAP S/4HANA ERP gateway across $N = 1{,}000$ multi-agent transactions per seed ($4{,}200$ total tool frames per run: $2{,}520$ [$60\%$] mutating frames and $1{,}680$ [$40\%$] read-only queries over a $|\mathcal{K}| = 64$ resource key-space under $\text{Zipf}(s = 1.15)$ contention, evaluated across $5$ independent random seeds [$5{,}000$ total transactions per baseline] with a controlled fault-injection budget of $148$ aborted transactions per seed [$14.80\%$, $0.0$ across-seed SD by design to isolate protocol behavior under a fixed fault rate, comprising $372$ aborted mutating frames per seed across the $148$ aborted transactions, or $\approx 2.51$ mutating frames/tx] and stochastic network/clock draws across seeds). The 6-agent hierarchical DAG topology comprises $1$ root orchestrator, $2$ tier-1 domain planners, and $3$ tier-2 execution subagents (depth $3$, max branching factor $2$, baseline $t_{\text{exec}} = 650.0 \pm 161.2\text{ ms}$ mean tool/agent execution path, which decomposes into $t_{\text{prep}} = 560.0 \pm 159.3\text{ ms}$ of upstream agent reasoning, tool synthesis, and staging plus $t_{\text{rtt}} = 90.0 \pm 24.5\text{ ms}$ of post-release final commit WAN RTT), where each transaction dispatches $1$ to $2$ exploratory read queries and $2$ to $3$ mutating tool calls (mean $4.20\text{ }\mbox{frames/transaction}$, with $46.6\%$ of transactions containing a two-step dependent same-key write chain [an intermediate preparatory mutation followed by a terminal settlement mutation] and $5.8\%$ [$97.4/1{,}680$] of reads issuing post-settlement verification queries during an active Phase~2 \texttt{PACED\_HELD} window on the same key), where intermediate non-terminal mutating writes inherit the speculative epoch $E$ of their enclosing transaction branch so all $2\text{--}3$ mutating frames per transaction are held in user-space \texttt{STAGE\_HELD} during Phase~1 and settled together upon completion of epoch $E$ (releasing in causal sequence order on sidecar-fronted links, or incurring one additional $90.0\text{ ms}$ sequential pipeline RTT prior to the terminal write across the $46.6\%$ chained transactions [$0.466 \times 90.0\text{ ms} = 42.0\text{ ms}$ amortized per transaction] on unmodified external SaaS endpoints). Network links were configured with Linux \texttt{tc netem} around a $90\text{ ms}$ base RTT ($45\text{ ms}$ one-way base delay with $\pm 30\text{ ms}$ independent uniform per-direction jitter, yielding $\text{Triangular}(-60\text{ ms}, 0\text{ ms}, +60\text{ ms})$ RTT jitter, strictly positive one-way transit $\in [15, 75]\text{ ms}$, and ingress sibling hold bound $\Delta_{\text{jitter\_hold}} = 60\text{ ms}$ matching the $75 - 15 = 60\text{ ms}$ max one-way spread; co-located regional verifiers use $\Delta_{\text{wan\_pre}} = 0\text{ ms}$), independent verifier and egress gateway TrueTime drift bounds $\epsilon_{\text{verifier}}, \epsilon_{\text{gateway}} \text{ i.i.d.}\sim \text{Uniform}(1.0\text{ ms}, 7.0\text{ ms})$ (whose sum $2\epsilon_{\text{eff}} = \epsilon_{\text{verifier}} + \epsilon_{\text{gateway}} \sim \text{Triangular}(2.0\text{ ms}, 8.0\text{ ms}, 14.0\text{ ms})$ has mean $8.00\text{ ms}$ and theoretical SD $\sqrt{6} \approx 2.45\text{ ms}$, with common-mode regional master reference $\delta_{\text{verifier}} - \delta_{\text{gateway}} = 0\text{ ms}$ [$\sigma_{\Delta\delta}^2 = 0\text{ ms}^2$] in Federated Egress Gateway Mode), upstream speculative verification durations $\Delta_{\text{stage}}$ clamped to $[200, 5{,}000]\text{ ms}$ from $\text{LogNormal}(\mu = 6.2, \sigma = 0.55)$ (theoretical unclamped median $492.7\text{ ms}$, $5$-seed empirical median $491.4\text{ ms}$, and clamped arithmetic mean $572.7 \pm 336.8\text{ ms}$), and sibling verifier completion offsets following $\Delta t_{\text{sib}} \sim \text{Normal}(\mu = 0\text{ ms}, \sigma = 22\text{ ms})$ around $T_E$---yielding a stochastic clock/network boundary split across the $148.0$ aborts of $119.0 \pm 1.1$ ($80.41\%$, $5$-seed mean $\pm$ SD) aborting during Phase~1 concurrent verification and $29.0 \pm 1.1$ ($19.59\%$) arriving within the $2\epsilon_{\text{eff}}$ cross-node clock-skew window during Phase~2. Because net intra-cluster settlement token transit minus abort margin ($X = \Delta_{\text{real\_ipc}} - \Delta_{\text{abort\_prop}} \sim \text{LogNormal}(\mu = 0.72, \sigma = 0.18)\text{ ms}$, mean $2.09 \pm 0.38\text{ ms}$ with Gaussian-copula load correlation $\rho = 0.42$ against $2\epsilon_{\text{eff}}$, co-located reference analytical variance $6.00 + 0.146 - 2(0.42)(2.449)(0.382) = 5.36\text{ ms}^2$ [$\text{SD} = 2.32\text{ ms}$], and $0.0\%$ zero-clamping since $\min(2\epsilon_{\text{eff}}) = 2.0\text{ ms}$) elapses before $t_{\text{now},\text{earliest}}$ is queried, the residual Phase~2 wait $\Delta_{\text{wait}}$ pooled over the $2{,}520 - 372 = 2{,}148$ non-aborted committed mutating frames per seed averages $5.91 \pm 2.32\text{ ms}$ ($p_{50} = 5.90\text{ ms}$, $p_{95} = 9.80\text{ ms}$, $p_{99} = 10.84\text{ ms}$; when sampling independent non-zero local clock offsets $\delta_u$ with $\sigma_{\Delta\delta} = 0.50\text{ ms}$ [$\sigma_{\Delta\delta}^2 = 0.25\text{ ms}^2$] across decoupled references, total analytical variance increases to $5.36 + 0.25 = 5.61\text{ ms}^2$ [$\text{SD} = 2.37\text{ ms}$], yielding $\Delta_{\text{wait}} = 5.91 \pm 2.37\text{ ms}$ with $p_{95} = 9.81\text{ ms}, p_{99} = 10.96\text{ ms}$). Transaction-level causal inversions are counted out of $N = 1{,}000$ transactions when any mutating or dependent frame arrives at the destination ERP ingress boundary ahead of a causally preceding frame ($\mathcal{V}_1 \prec_{\mathcal{V}} \mathcal{V}_2$) or out of physical commit order across concurrent conflicting writes.

\section{Conclusion and Operational Scope}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Agent-Chronos establishes a hardware-anchored L7 transport fencing and speculative pacing protocol for cross-cloud multi-agent swarms. By combining two-phase user-space speculative staging, TrueTime commit-wait serialization ($\Delta_{\text{wait}} = \max(0, t_{F,\text{latest}} + \Delta_{\text{abort\_prop}} - t_{\text{now},\text{earliest}})$), decoupled swarm-wide causal vectors and per-destination sequence coordinates, and ingress jitter re-sequencing, Agent-Chronos eliminates phantom tool mutations and causal inversions across Chronos-enabled sidecar gateways while delivering a $423.3\text{ ms}$ ($34.6\%$) net speculative latency reduction per committed transaction ($388.5\text{ ms}$, or $31.8\%$, unconditionally across all transactions) without holding distributed database locks. Functionally, Agent-Chronos guarantees strict client-side egress linearizability and end-to-end causal delivery across sidecar-fronted endpoints rather than internal database strict serializability; when invoking unmodified third-party SaaS APIs that lack an ingress sidecar, the gateway suppresses tombstone sequence ACKs, routes concurrent sibling writes ($\mathcal{V}_1 \parallel \mathcal{V}_2$) through a shared logical egress gateway, and falls back to sequential per-resource in-flight pipelining ($+42.0\text{ ms}$ mean per-transaction overhead) paired with compensating sagas for post-transmission HTTP 5xx faults. Operational deployment assumes access to TrueTime, local \texttt{/dev/ptp0} hardware clocks, or a federated Chronos egress gateway, alongside bounded active swarm fan-out ($|\text{AgentID}| \le 16$, or hierarchical cluster watermarks for $>100$-agent meshes). Future work will extend Agent-Chronos to dynamic multi-tier cluster reconfiguration and hardware NIC offload of L7 TrueTime pacing.
